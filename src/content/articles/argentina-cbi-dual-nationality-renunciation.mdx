Argentina CBI dual nationality rules do not state that an applicant must surrender an existing citizenship. The reviewed Argentine sources also do not set out a final, CBI-specific rule on renunciation. Applicants should therefore avoid treating the silence as a guaranteed answer for every case, and check the law of each nationality they already hold.

As of November 11, 2026.

## What Argentine law says

Argentina's current [Citizenship Law 346](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-346-48854/actualizacion) provides for naturalization, including a route based on a "relevant investment" added by [Decree 366/2025](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/norma-413297). The investment provision does not say that an applicant must renounce another nationality. Nor does the reviewed text set out a CBI-specific renunciation declaration or procedure. That is a limit of what the published provisions establish, not proof that no later application rule will address the issue.

There is related guidance for Argentine nationality more broadly. An [Argentine consular page on choosing citizenship](https://cmila.cancilleria.gob.ar/es/node/4639) states that native or acquired Argentine nationality is "irremissible," and that a purported renunciation affects the exercise of political rights rather than the nationality itself. The page refers to Article 16 of [Decree 3213/1984](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/norma-25471/texto). This provides useful context about Argentina's approach to its own nationality. It is not a final CBI procedure, and that consular page was last updated in 2015, before the investment route was added.

The careful conclusion is narrower than "Argentina guarantees dual citizenship in every situation." The sources reviewed do not show an Argentine requirement to renounce another nationality as a condition of CBI naturalization. They also do not provide a final, program-specific answer for applicants or accompanying relatives. The rules and forms issued for operating applications could clarify what documents are required. Any such detail should be checked against the current official instructions when applications are accepted.

## Your other country's law is a separate question

Argentina's treatment of its nationality cannot decide whether a person retains citizenship of another country. Each country sets its own rules. Some countries permit multiple nationalities. Others may restrict them or attach consequences to voluntary acquisition of a foreign nationality. The relevant question depends on the applicant's exact citizenship status, not just where they were born or live.

Before applying, check the other country's official nationality law and guidance from its responsible government agency or consulate. Ask specifically whether naturalization in Argentina triggers loss, requires a declaration, or affects particular rights or obligations. If an applicant has more than one existing nationality, assess each one separately. A spouse or child included in a family application should also receive an individual review. Their citizenship may be governed by different rules, including rules that apply to minors or to a parent's actions.

Do not rely on a general statement about "dual citizenship" from an adviser or a program summary when the home-country law is decisive. Obtain advice from a lawyer or qualified nationality specialist familiar with that country's current rules. Keep written confirmation where possible, especially before signing any declaration or taking a formal oath.

## Program status and practical checks

The government's announcement, reported by [Reuters on October 2, 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/argentina-announces-citizenship-by-investment-program-2026-10-02/), said applications were expected to open in the fourth quarter of 2026. As of November 11, 2026, the reviewed sources do not confirm that applications are open. The [program status page](/research/argentina-citizenship-by-investment-status) tracks developments, but applicants should verify launch status and final requirements through official Argentine channels before acting.

For now, treat renunciation as an unresolved program-specific detail. Confirm Argentine instructions once published, and separately confirm the consequences under every other nationality you hold. Citizenship and tax residence are different matters. Acquiring a nationality alone does not settle where someone is tax-resident.
