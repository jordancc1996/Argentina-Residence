Argentina CBI family eligibility is only partly set out in the October 2, 2026 announcement. The announcement names family categories for the citizenship-by-investment program, but leaves important edge cases unresolved. It says a principal applicant's spouse and children may apply if they prove the relationship and make the stated contribution. It lists children under 18 at USD 25,000, and unmarried children aged 18 to 25 who have no children of their own at USD 100,000. It does not define "child," explain how age is measured, or set out rules for adoption, stepchildren, custody, or dependency. Those details should not be assumed. The [official announcement](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/noticias/luis-caputo-anuncio-la-puesta-en-marcha-del-programa-de-ciudadania-por-inversion-de) states the announced categories and amounts.

As of November 11, 2026, the government has said applications are expected during the fourth quarter, but has not confirmed an open filing channel. [KPMG likewise reported](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-258.html) on October 8 that the opening date and channel were unconfirmed. Check current government instructions before filing or transferring funds. The [Argentina CBI family FAQ](/faq/argentina-citizenship-investment-family) summarizes the published categories, not final rules for these edge cases.

## Adopted children: relationship proof is not a complete rule

The announcement requires proof of the relevant family relationship. It does not say whether a child adopted under a foreign order, a local order, or a particular adoption type qualifies. Nor does it specify which authority must recognize an adoption, whether a final judgment is needed, or what records and translations will be accepted.

[Decree 524/2025](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/norma-415710/texto) sets an administrative process for the principal investment applicant and directs the Citizenship by Investment Programs Agency to issue supplementary or clarifying rules. It does not settle the family definitions in the later announcement. Families should wait for those rules before treating adoption records as sufficient or paying for a child.

## Stepchildren and blended families

The announcement refers to the spouse and children of the principal applicant. It does not state whether a spouse's child from an earlier relationship is included, whether the principal applicant must adopt that child, or whether the child's other legal parent must consent. Marriage to a child's parent should not be treated as proof of CBI eligibility unless the implementing rules say so.

The [updated Citizenship Law 346](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/norma-48854/actualizacion) creates the investment-based naturalization route, but does not describe stepchildren as CBI applicants. General family migration rules address different processes and should not be used to fill gaps in the investment program's terms.

## Custody, consent, and adult dependency

The announcement does not explain how to handle shared or sole custody, an unavailable parent, or a dispute between guardians. It also does not say whether both parents must consent to a minor's application, who must sign, or when a court order is needed. Do not assume that authority for a passport or travel decision answers the separate CBI application question.

The age bands leave another gap. The announcement identifies children under 18 and a defined group aged 18 to 25: unmarried and without children of their own. It does not announce a broader exception for an adult child who relies financially on the principal applicant because of disability, education, or another circumstance. "Dependent" is not a separate announced category. Whether an adult could qualify under any eventual exception remains unknown.

## What if a child turns 18 while the case is pending?

The announcement does not say whether age is measured on the filing date, when an application is accepted as complete, at decision, or on another date. The announcement does not state when unmarried status and having no children are tested.

The decree gives the National Directorate of Migration 30 business days to decide after receiving the Agency's report. That is not a total processing deadline from initial filing, and the decree supplies no age-lock rule. Families should seek written guidance from the competent authority once detailed filing rules appear, rather than plan around an assumed birthday cutoff.

## Wait for the rules that answer these questions

The government has announced the main family categories and contributions. It has not resolved how they apply to adoption, stepchildren, parental authority, adult dependency, or a birthday during processing. Rely on final program regulations and official filing instructions, not informal interpretations. Consult a qualified Argentine immigration and family-law professional about individual circumstances.
