Argentine law contains a naturalization pathway for a foreign national who makes an investment that the Ministry of Economy defines as relevant. DNU 366/2025 created APCI. Decree 524/2025 subsequently established a basic review procedure involving APCI. The decrees do not publish an operational due diligence manual or an application-document checklist. The pathway should not be treated as an open application program unless the government publishes the applicable criteria and confirms that applications are being accepted.

The research note on the [Argentina citizenship by investment launch date](/research/argentina-citizenship-by-investment-launch-date) records that the published decrees do not define application procedures or due diligence protocols. This page does not treat a tender cancellation as an established fact.

## What the Due Diligence Process Is Expected to Involve

The decrees describe a multi-agency review rather than a single-office rubber stamp. APCI, under the Ministry of Economy, is expected to receive the investment proposal and coordinate background work. Existing program summaries on this site list Immigration, security authorities, the Financial Intelligence Unit (UIF), and the intelligence secretariat (SIDE) as the other bodies in that chain.

## Source of Funds Documentation

Source of funds requirements on this site are already stated at a category level. [Investment requirements](/faq/argentina-citizenship-investment-requirements) say all investment amounts must be funded from lawfully sourced capital originating outside Argentina, with bank statements, tax filings, and source-of-wealth declarations. That is documentation of origin and path, not a second investment quota.

Expected source-of-funds files, based on those categories, include:

<ChecklistCard
  heading="Expected source-of-funds files"
  items={[
    "Bank statements showing the movement of funds. The decrees do not identify the destination asset.",
    "Tax filings that reconcile the same funds with declared income or gains in the country where the wealth was taxed.",
    "A source-of-wealth narrative that explains how the applicant accumulated the capital (employment, company sale, inheritance, investment returns), supported by contracts, sale agreements, or estate documents where they exist.",
    "Corporate records if the funds move through a company: ownership, dividend or loan paperwork, and evidence that the company could legally distribute the money.",
  ]}
/>

The decrees do not set a confirmed minimum amount. See the [investment requirements](/faq/argentina-citizenship-investment-requirements). Those reported figures are not a source-of-funds form and are not current qualifying requirements. Source-of-funds review asks where capital came from and how it moved.

APCI has not published a list of accepted banks, a look-back period, or a template declaration. Preparing the categories above is file hygiene. It is not a substitute for the forms that do not yet exist. Latest framework notes are in the [Decree 524/2025 progress update](/industry-news/decree-524-2025-progress-update).

## Net Worth Requirements

Net worth requirements have not been published as a separate numeric floor. They are not a statement that applicants must show a multiple of the anticipated path amounts as personal net worth.

Source of funds and net worth are related but not identical:

- Source of funds traces the specific capital used for the Argentine investment.
- Source of wealth, and any net-worth review, looks at whether the applicant's overall resources are consistent with that capital.

Until APCI issues a net-worth schedule, there is no official Argentine number to target. What can be assembled now is evidence that the applicant's declared wealth matches the source-of-funds story: audited financials or tax summaries, property and company registries, and an explanation of liabilities. Those materials support consistency. They are not a published net-worth test.

A personal-net-worth rule of thumb imported from another program is not an Argentine requirement. Do not treat marketing rules of thumb from other citizenship programs as Argentine law.

## Argentina Citizenship by Investment for Crypto Investors

The decrees do not identify cryptocurrency or another digital asset as a qualifying or disqualifying investment. [Investment requirements](/faq/argentina-citizenship-investment-requirements) records that gap. Holding Bitcoin, ether, or tokens is not stated here as the investment.

The decrees also do not say that fiat proceeds from a prior crypto sale qualify or disqualify. APCI has not published a crypto-specific source-of-funds protocol.

Documentation crypto-derived capital is expected to need, if and when filings open:

<ChecklistCard
  heading="Crypto-derived capital trail"
  items={[
    "Exchange account statements showing acquisition, disposal, and fiat withdrawal, including dates and counterparties.",
    "Wallet records that can be tied to the same identity as the passport on the file, where funds never sat on a licensed exchange.",
    "Tax filings in the jurisdiction that taxed the disposal, matching the fiat amount later used in Argentina.",
    "Bank statements covering fiat received from an exchange or OTC desk. The decrees do not identify the later use of that fiat.",
    "An explanation of mixing, privacy wallets, or unhosted chains if those appear in the history. Gaps in the trail are the usual failure point in source-of-funds files, not the fact that the original asset was crypto.",
  ]}
/>

Mining, staking, or protocol income would be expected to sit in the source-of-wealth narrative (how the coins were earned), then in the same conversion trail. There is no published Argentine shortcut that treats a wallet screenshot as source of funds.

Until APCI writes the protocol, crypto investors can organize exchange, tax, and bank records. They cannot submit those records to an application program. The pathway should not be treated as open unless the government confirms that applications are being accepted.
