Decree 524/2025 does not set a dollar amount for Argentina. Secondary reports have described a $500,000 Treasury contribution and a $1,000,000 seven-year 0% bond. Those figures were not found in an enacted regulation. They are not current qualifying requirements. Applications are not open.

Panama's Qualified Investor route provides permanent residency, not citizenship. [Panama's Ministry of Commerce and Industries](https://mici.gob.pa/2026/09/21/atraccion-de-inversion-e-impulso-al-empleo-panama-amplia-el-alcance-del-programa-inversionista-calificado/) states that Executive Decree 17 of 8 September 2026 replaced the 2020 rule. A first-sale new property qualifies at B/.300,000. A second-sale property qualifies at B/.500,000. A fixed-term deposit at Banco Nacional de Panamá or Caja de Ahorros qualifies at B/.500,000. The same release says securities routes were expanded. It does not state a securities minimum or a private-bank minimum, so those amounts are not restated here. Citizenship requires a separate naturalization process.

## Argentina Citizenship by Investment Expected Requirements

Decree 366/2025 and Decree 524/2025 are in the Official Gazette. Decree 524/2025 does not set a dollar amount.

Secondary reports, which are not an enacted schedule, have described:

- A $500,000 contribution to the Argentine Treasury; or
- A $1,000,000 investment in a 7-year, 0% Argentine government bond, with principal described as returned at maturity.

Those figures are not current qualifying requirements.

A spouse and children under 18 are expected to be includable. The position for dependants aged 18 and older remains unknown. See [investment requirements](/faq/argentina-citizenship-investment-requirements).

A cancelled Argentine consultancy tender described 5,000 APCI recommendation reports. That is not an application cap. Decree 524/2025 does not require a biometrics visit or set a residence quota.

Processing time remains unknown. These terms remain preliminary until Argentina publishes the final program requirements and application procedures. For the Argentine program structure independent of this comparison, see the [Argentina Golden Visa Program](/guides/argentina-golden-visa-program) guide.

## How Does Panama Residency by Investment Work?

<ComparisonBlock id="panama" />

Panama already operates several immigration routes for foreign investors, including the Friendly Nations Visa and Qualified Investor Visa.

The Friendly Nations route is available to qualifying nationalities that establish an economic connection to Panama. Qualifying options have included employment or an investment such as $200,000 in real estate or a fixed-term bank deposit.

The Qualified Investor Visa is a separate permanent residence route designed for larger investments. Confirm the live country list and economic-tie schedule with Panamanian counsel. This page covers Panama only. For the Mercosur-member investor-residence comparison, see [Argentina citizenship by investment vs Paraguay Investor Pass](/guides/argentina-citizenship-investment-vs-paraguay).

## How Much Does the Panama Qualified Investor Visa Require?

Panama's Qualified Investor Visa provides several investment options.

The Ministry of Commerce release of 21 September 2026 does not describe a future increase on 15 October 2026. It distinguishes B/.300,000 for new first-sale property from B/.500,000 for second-sale property, and B/.500,000 for a fixed-term deposit at Banco Nacional de Panamá or Caja de Ahorros. A securities minimum and a private-bank minimum of $750,000 are not stated in that release.

Investors should confirm the current thresholds and holding requirements before proceeding.

## Does Panama Residency Lead to Citizenship?

Panama investor programs do not provide citizenship when the investment is approved. They provide residence status.

Naturalization is a separate process. Foreign nationals may generally become eligible after five years of permanent residence, subject to requirements that include Spanish-language ability, good conduct, and government approval.

Argentina's proposed program differs because it is being developed as a route to citizenship through qualifying investment, rather than residency followed by naturalization.

## Is Argentina Citizenship by Investment Open?

No. Argentina's program is not currently open for applications.

Decree 524/2025 established the framework for investment-based citizenship and created the relevant investment citizenship agency, but final investment requirements, application procedures, due-diligence standards, and processing timelines have not yet been published.

Official government links are on [Argentina residency resources](/resources). See [application process timeline](/faq/argentina-citizenship-investment-application-timeline) and [residency requirements](/faq/argentina-residency-physical-presence).

## Argentina vs Panama: What Is the Main Difference?

Panama offers established investment-based residency programs that are operating now, with citizenship potentially available later through a separate naturalization process.

Argentina is developing a different model: citizenship through qualifying investment without an anticipated residence requirement. However, applications are not yet open, and the expected investment terms remain subject to Argentina's final implementation rules.
