Argentina citizenship by investment vs the Greece Golden Visa compares a proposed direct citizenship framework with an established European residence-by-investment program.

Argentina's citizenship by investment framework is being developed as a direct route to Argentine citizenship through qualifying investment. Expected options include a $500,000 non-refundable contribution to the Argentine Treasury or a $1 million investment in a 7-year, 0% Argentine government bond, with the principal expected to be returned at maturity. No residence requirement is anticipated. Applications are not yet open, and final program terms have not been published.

The Greece Golden Visa provides a renewable five-year residence permit rather than direct citizenship. Real estate investment starts at €400,000 in most of Greece and €800,000 in areas including Attica, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini, and certain larger islands. A €250,000 threshold applies to defined property-conversion and restoration exceptions. Greece's program is operating and accepting applications.

## How Does the Greece Golden Visa Work?

The Greece Golden Visa provides a five-year renewable residence permit for qualifying investors and included family members. The investment must generally remain in place for the residence permit to be renewed.

The permit allows the holder to reside in Greece and provides short-stay travel within the Schengen Area. It does not provide Greek citizenship or a Greek passport.

## How Much Do You Need to Invest in Greek Real Estate?

Greece uses location-based real estate investment thresholds.

A minimum €800,000 investment applies in Attica, including Athens, as well as Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini, and islands with more than 3,100 inhabitants. The threshold is €400,000 in other areas of Greece.

A €250,000 threshold applies to specific exceptions, including qualifying commercial-to-residential conversions and restoration of listed buildings. It is not a general €250,000 real estate option throughout Greece.

## Does the Greece Golden Visa Require You to Live in Greece?

There is no minimum physical-presence requirement to maintain or renew the Golden Visa, according to the program information summarized on this page.

However, maintaining a Golden Visa is different from qualifying for Greek citizenship.

## Does the Greece Golden Visa Lead to Citizenship?

Greek citizenship requires a separate naturalization process. The Golden Visa itself does not provide nationality.

Greek naturalization is commonly based on seven years of legal residence and includes physical-presence, language, and integration requirements. Holding a Golden Visa while primarily living outside Greece does not by itself satisfy those citizenship requirements.

## Is Argentina Citizenship by Investment Open?

No. Argentina is not currently accepting citizenship-by-investment applications.

The proposed framework is intended to provide Argentine nationality through qualifying investment rather than a renewable residence permit. Final application procedures, due-diligence protocols, processing times, and operational rules remain pending.

## Argentina vs Greece: What Is the Main Difference?

Greece offers an operating residence-by-investment program, with real estate thresholds generally ranging from €400,000 to €800,000 and a €250,000 route for defined exceptions. Investors receive a renewable residence permit, not immediate citizenship.

Argentina is developing a direct citizenship framework based on an anticipated $500,000 Treasury contribution or $1 million government bond. Unlike Greece, real estate is not currently an anticipated qualifying investment. Argentina's program, however, is not yet open for applications.
