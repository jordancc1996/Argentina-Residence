Argentina citizenship by investment vs Caribbean citizenship compares Argentina's proposed framework with operating citizenship-by-investment programs in Dominica, Grenada, St. Kitts and Nevis, Antigua and Barbuda, and Saint Lucia.

Argentina's citizenship by investment framework is being developed as a direct route to Argentine citizenship. Expected options include a $500,000 non-refundable contribution to the Argentine Treasury or a $1 million investment in a 7-year, 0% Argentine government bond, with the principal expected to be returned at maturity. No residence requirement is anticipated. Applications are not yet open, and final program terms have not been published.

Caribbean CBI programs are already operating. Dominica starts at a $200,000 government contribution or $200,000 approved real estate investment. Grenada's National Transformation Fund contribution starts at $235,000, with an approved real estate route also available. St. Kitts and Nevis starts at a $250,000 government contribution, while approved developer real estate starts at $325,000. Antigua and Barbuda starts at a $230,000 National Development Fund contribution or $300,000 approved real estate investment. Saint Lucia's National Economic Fund contribution is $240,000 for an applicant with up to three qualifying dependants.

## How Much Does Caribbean Citizenship by Investment Cost?

Dominica offers one of the lower entry points, with a $200,000 Economic Diversification Fund contribution for a single applicant or $250,000 for a main applicant with up to three qualifying dependants. Approved real estate starts at $200,000.

Grenada's National Transformation Fund contribution starts at $235,000 and can cover a family of up to four. Approved real estate is another qualifying route.

St. Kitts and Nevis requires at least $250,000 through its Sustainable Island State Contribution. Approved developer real estate starts at $325,000.

Antigua and Barbuda's National Development Fund starts at $230,000. Approved real estate requires at least $300,000, while the University of the West Indies Fund option is $260,000.

Saint Lucia's National Economic Fund requires $240,000 for an applicant with up to three qualifying dependants.

## Do Caribbean CBI Programs Provide Citizenship Directly?

Yes. These programs provide citizenship following approval, due diligence, and completion of the qualifying investment. They are not residence permits that require investors to wait several years before applying for naturalization.

Physical residence requirements are generally limited or absent, although individual programs can impose specific requirements. Antigua and Barbuda, for example, requires citizens under its CBI program to spend at least five days in the country during the first five calendar years after obtaining citizenship.

## How Does Argentina Differ from Caribbean CBI?

Argentina's anticipated investment amounts are higher, but its proposed $1 million government bond has a different structure from a non-refundable Caribbean contribution because the principal is expected to be returned after seven years.

Argentina is also expected to provide citizenship without a prior residence period. Unlike the Caribbean programs, however, Argentina is not currently accepting applications and its final operational rules have not been published.

## Which Caribbean CBI Programs Are Open?

Dominica, Grenada, St. Kitts and Nevis, Antigua and Barbuda, and Saint Lucia have established citizenship-by-investment programs. Argentina's proposed investment citizenship framework remains under development.

The key difference today is therefore availability: Caribbean applicants can apply under established program rules, while prospective Argentina applicants must wait for the final framework and application process.
